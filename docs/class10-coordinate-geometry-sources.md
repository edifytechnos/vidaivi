Question 3 is worth reading closely: its Reason is **false**, and checking that
was the work. The point $(-3, k)$ divides $(-5,4)$ and $(-2,3)$ in the ratio
$2 : 1$, not the $1 : 2$ the Reason claims — so the key is (C), assertion true
and reason false.

## Untagged questions

Questions **4**, **5**, **6**, **9** and **15** carry no year: distance from the
origin, a point on the $x$-axis equidistant from two others, a plain distance
calculation, finding $y$ from a given distance, and collinearity by zero area.
Ordinary NCERT Chapter 7 material, written to fill the one-mark slots.

## Changes of form, stated plainly

- Questions **7** and **8** are 3-mark and 2-mark written questions in the
  papers; here they are numeric entry asking for the length to two decimal
  places ($\sqrt{10} \to 3.16$, $\frac{5\sqrt{5}}{2} \to 5.59$), marked with a
  tolerance of $0.01$.
- Question **10** is one half of a paper question with an OR; question **11** is
  the other half. Both are used, each keeping its own 2 marks.
- Maths is re-typeset in KaTeX. No question's content was changed.

Every answer was recomputed independently in Python: the ratios $2:7$, $1:2$,
$5:1$ and $8:7$; the medians $\sqrt{10}$ and $\frac{5\sqrt{5}}{2}$; and the
isosceles triangle's sides $5$, $5\sqrt{2}$, $5$ — which also make it
right-angled, a detail the solution points out.
