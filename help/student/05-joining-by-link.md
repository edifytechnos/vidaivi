## What you get

Some teachers do not hand out a username and a password. They send a **link**
and read out — or send — an **access code** that looks like `ABC-DEF`. You need
both: the link opens the test's name, and the code is what lets you in.

## Joining

1. Open the link. You see the test's name, your teacher's name, and how many
   questions and marks it has.
2. Press **Continue with Google** and pick the Google account you want to use.
   Your teacher will see the name and the email on it — nobody else does.
3. Type the access code. It is six letters; the dash is put in for you, and
   capitals do not matter.
4. Press **Join and open the test**.

If it says the code is not right, check it with your teacher — it is easy to
mishear a letter. Guesses are counted, and too many in a row locks you out
for a while.

If it says the test is *not taking more students*, your teacher's account is
full. Ask them; there is nothing to fix on your side.

## After that

You are a student of that teacher, signed in on this phone for about a month.
Your **subjects** show the tests you have joined — only those, not everything
the teacher has written. A second link from the same teacher adds its test to
the same account; just open it and type its code.

If the test is *not open yet*, your teacher has not published it. You have
joined; come back when they say it is open.

To join from another phone, open the same link there and sign in with the same
Google account. Your papers and marks are on the account, not the phone.

## Signed in as somebody else?

If the link says you are signed in as a teacher or a parent, somebody else used
this phone. Press **Sign out and continue** — their session ends and the link
opens again for you.
