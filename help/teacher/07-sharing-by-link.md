## When to use it

Issuing a username and a password to each student is right for a school class
you teach all year. It is the wrong amount of work for a batch you meet once, a
coaching group that changes every month, or a college class of two hundred who
all have Gmail already. For those, share the test by **link**.

Anyone with the link **and** the access code signs in with Google, types the
code, and the test opens. From then on they are a student of yours — in your
**Students** list, in **Results**, in the marking queue — exactly like one you
issued a login to.

## Switching it on

**Share by link** is the link icon in the editor's action row, and on a test's
row in My tests. Press **Turn on** and you get two things:

- **The link** — `?join=` and a long token. It names the test and nothing else.
- **The access code** — six letters, shown as `ABC-DEF`. Read it out in class,
  or send it with the link.

**Copy message with link and code** gives you one WhatsApp message with both.

> The code is the lock. A link forwarded on its own opens the test's name and
> size and a code box, and goes no further. If the code gets out, press **New
> code**: the old one stops working for anyone who has not joined yet, and
> everyone who already joined keeps the test.

**Turn off** stops new people joining. The link and the code are kept, so
switching on again next week needs no new message. Students who joined while it
was on keep the test either way.

## What the student sees

The test's name, its subtitle, your name, and how many questions and marks.
Then **Continue with Google**, then the code box. The code box stays shut until
Google has answered, so there is nothing to get wrong in the wrong order.

A test that is not yet **published** can still be joined — the screen says *not
open yet* and the student comes back when it is. Publish it as you would any
other test.

## Who they are in your account

A student who joined by link shows in **Students** by their **email**, with
*joined by link* under it, and has no password to reset — Google is their
sign-in. They see **only the tests they joined with a code**, not everything
you publish; sharing a second test with the same person adds it to what they
can see, on the same account.

They take a seat like any other student. {{rules.teacherFreeStudents}} are
included; more are paid seats. When your seats are full, the link tells the
student the test is not taking more people and to ask you — it never names a
price to them.

A student of yours who is already signed in needs only the code; a test they
have joined already simply opens. Somebody signed in as a teacher or a parent
is asked to sign out first, so a session is never replaced mid-sitting.

## Results, and releasing to everyone who sat it

**Results** (the chart icon beside Share by link) is one screen for one test:
who took it, when, the score, and **Open & mark** on each row. The counts and
the average are over **handed-in** papers only; a student still part-way
through shows with no score and *See what they have so far*.

**Release to everyone** opens the paper — marks, correct answers and worked
solutions — to everybody who took it, and to anyone who takes it later. **Hide
again** shuts it. It is the same release as [releasing the
answers](06-releasing.html), reached from the one screen that lists everyone
who sat this test.
