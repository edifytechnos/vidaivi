## What is in the library

{{library.shelves}} subjects, {{library.tests}} chapter tests,
{{library.questions}} questions — fifteen per chapter, written to the board's own
pattern.

{{library.table}}

## Browsing before you take

**Browse tests** is read-only, three levels deep: subjects → that subject's
chapters → one chapter, one question at a time with its worked solution. Read a
test properly before you put it in front of a class; that is what this screen is
for.

## Taking a copy

Two places offer it, both at a moment you are already asking for something:

- **New subject** → step 1 offers the ready-made subjects, step 2 is a tick list
  of that subject's chapters. Tick several — you take a term's worth in one go.
- **The + in the tests tree**, inside a subject you already have, offers to copy
  one built-in test into it.

Or **Use this test** on a Browse row, which files the copy under your matching
subject and opens it.

## What a copy is

**Yours.** A new test, in your account, as a **draft**, with every question
carried across. Edit it, delete questions, add your own, change the marks.

Two things follow from that:

- **Nothing syncs afterwards.** If the original is corrected later, your copy is
  not. Your copy is your copy.
- **You cannot change the original.** Only an admin edits the library.

## The free allowance and the price

You may take **{{rules.freeShelfTests}} tests** free. Past that, taking more from
a subject costs {{rules.subjectPaise.rupees}} for the whole subject — every
chapter in it, once, no subscription. The refusal names the price and offers the
payment dialog there and then.

Once you own a subject, copy from it as freely as you like.

## Writing a subject of your own

**New subject** → **Something else**, then board, class and subject. An empty
subject is a perfectly good start; add tests with the **+** in the tree.

## Where did my copies go?

They are drafts in the subject you filed them under, and a banner in the editor
says how many arrived. A draft reaches nobody until you publish it — see
[writing a test](03-writing-a-test.html).
