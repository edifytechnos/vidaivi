## The editor

Three columns: your **tests** down the left, the **question** in the middle, the
**explanation** on the right. Below a tablet the explanation drops under the
question and the tree becomes a drawer behind the **Questions** button.

Every test is a root in that tree and its questions are the level beneath, so you
can move between papers without leaving the screen.

- **New test** is the small **+** in the tree's header.
- **New question** is the **+** that appears between two rows when you hover —
  it inserts at exactly that point.
- Each question row has a **…** menu (duplicate, move, delete) and a drag handle.

Everything saves itself about a second after you stop typing. There is no Save
button and nothing to lose.

## Title and subtitle

A test is named on two lines wherever it appears: **Title** on top, **Subtitle**
underneath. Nothing is derived or rearranged — line one is what you type in
Title, line two is what you type in Subtitle. Put the paper's name in the first
and the chapter in the second.

## The four things a question can be

| Type | The student does | Marked by |
|---|---|---|
| Multiple choice | Taps an option | Instantly |
| Short answer | Types a number or a symbol | Instantly where it can be read; otherwise it comes to you |
| Long answer | Photographs their working | You |

**Mark the correct option.** The radio beside an option *is* the answer, and
nothing is pre-selected. Until you pick one, the panel says publishing is
blocked — that is better than a paper where A is silently the answer to
everything.

**Short answers may be symbols.** Put the answer in however it is properly
written, and use **Also accept** for the other reasonable spellings. What is not
recognised comes to your marking queue and is **never** counted wrong.

Write the **worked solution** on every question. It is what the student reads
after release, and on a board paper the method is the thing.

## Source

Optional, beside Topic — *CBSE 2024*, say. It shows as a quiet chip next to the
question. Only tag a year you have actually seen the paper for: a wrong year in
front of a class is worse than no year.

## Formatting

In question text and solutions you have maths between dollar signs, `**bold**`,
and a blank line for a paragraph break. That is all — **no tables, no bullet
lists, no HTML**. A markdown table reaches the student as a wall of pipe
characters. For a frequency table, write one line per row with the header in
bold.

## Publishing

A draft reaches nobody, however it is assigned. Publishing is the act of sharing.

- Check the paper first with **Preview**.
- **Publish** is in the Test details panel's own header, with Who sees this and
  Preview.
- If a question is unfinished, publishing is refused and names every question
  that needs work.

## Editing a published test

You cannot, directly — a published paper is being read by students and changing
it under them is the one thing that must not happen. **Move back to draft**,
edit, publish again.

If publishing is refused because somebody is part-way through, the message names
them. That is often **you**, from your own preview left open. Discard that
in-progress paper from the same message — but note that discarding a *student's*
throws away the answers they have written, so only do it for a paper that has
genuinely been abandoned.
