## Where marking happens

Inside the paper. **To mark** is a list — one card per student and test, *"3 to
mark"* — and opening one gives you that student's whole paper in the read-only
review screen, with a marks row under each written answer.

You get there from the queue, or from **Open & mark** on a row in a student's
report.

That is deliberate: the multiple choice and the clear short answers are already
graded and already showing their marks. Marking three loose long answers without
seeing which MCQs the class got wrong is marking with your eyes shut.

## Reading the handwriting

Tap a photograph and it opens full screen, black, with zoom, pan and rotate. A
page photographed sideways is the normal case, not an edge one, so **r** rotates.
`+` `-` `0` zoom, the arrows move between that answer's pages, Escape closes.

Do not award a mark off a thumbnail. You cannot see a minus sign in pencil at
that size.

## Awarding

Type the mark, add a comment if it helps, save. The question list and the total
update as you go, so on a phone — where the list is behind a button — you can
tell it worked without reopening anything.

You never write to the student's own paper. Their auto-graded subtotal stays as
it is and your marks are added on top wherever the score is shown.

## Assess with AI

**Assess with AI** on a written answer reads it and proposes a mark, a comment
and its reasoning.

**It never awards anything.** The proposal is deliberately *not* typed into the
box for you — a number you have to choose gets reviewed, and a number already
sitting there gets rubber-stamped. Read it, then put in your own.

It works on photographed long answers and on typed short answers alike. It costs
one credit per answer, runs only when you press it, and it is never told who the
student is — it gets the question, your model solution, the marks available and
the answer. Nothing else.

If the button is missing, AI marking is not switched on for this platform.

## Credits

Your balance appears beside the marks row and is seeded when the paper opens, so
a warning arrives before you spend rather than after. Below a fifth of your grant
it turns amber.

Out of credits, **marking by hand still works** — everything does. Ask an admin
for a top-up.

## Short answers that came to you

An answer the grader had no rule for arrives in the queue rather than being
counted wrong. Usually it is a symbol written a way you had not listed. Award the
mark, and consider adding that spelling to the question's **Also accept** so the
next class is marked automatically.
