## What release does

A signed-in student who hands in sees **nothing**: no verdict, no correct answer,
no worked solution, and no score. They can reopen the paper and read what they
put down, and that is all.

**Release** opens it. From then on they see their marks, the correct answers and
the worked solution beside every question, and may retake it.

## Why it works that way

Two reasons, and both matter in front of a class:

- A score with the long answers unmarked is a half-truth, and a student will read
  it as their result.
- You decide when a class discusses a paper. Answers leaking to whoever finishes
  first is how a test stops being a test.

## Pressing it

- **For one student** — the **Release** button in the crumb row of their paper,
  or from their report.
- **For the whole class** — release without naming a student, from the marking
  queue or the test's own row.

**Unrelease** shuts it again if you released early.

## You are never gated by it

You see every answer as soon as it is handed in. Release is your switch, not a
lock on you — you have to read the paper before deciding to open it.

## What a student sees before release

In their results list: *"Handed in 14 Sep · waiting for your teacher"*. Which is
true, and is the sentence that stops them asking.

## Guests are different

Somebody sitting the public demo with no account gets the old instant feedback —
verdict, solution and score. They have no teacher to release anything, and the
demo has to stay worth sharing.
